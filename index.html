Reading the labels

“Free” can mean three different things.

Many programs are free to learn from but charge for the certificate. We keep those two facts apart on every card.

Free to enroll

You can start without paying

Lessons and materials are open at no cost. This says nothing yet about the certificate.

Free certificate

The credential costs nothing

You can earn the certificate or digital credential without paying, once you meet the stated requirements.

Free with conditions

There is a catch to check

For example a proctored exam fee, an application for financial aid, a regional limit or a sign-up window.

A real example: NPTEL courses are free to join and learn from, but the certificate needs an optional, paid, proctored exam. That is why NPTEL is labelled “free to enroll” and “paid exam for certificate”.

How we verify

Every listing shows its checking status.

We compare each entry with the provider’s own pages where we can and say so when we can’t. We don’t invent certificates, partnerships or statistics. Always confirm terms on the official page before you enroll or apply.

  • Checked against official source — key terms were confirmed on the provider’s own page on the date shown.
  • Partly verified — some details came from secondary sources or older official pages. Confirm before relying on them.
  • Not yet verified — listed for convenience; we have not re-checked it.
  • Expired or closed — kept visible so you know it is no longer available.
